# Analysis — oriented_cyl (exp_06)

## Outcome

**The pre-registered positive conclusion holds: the orientation-conditioned cylindrical pipeline removes the hallway C50 deficit of the CylindricalViT on the real rooms, and it does so through the orientation channel, not through the heading frame.** Gate G1 passed on the pretrained checkpoint (hallway mirror cosine −0.156 for the oriented model against 0.944 for the exp_01 cylindrical model and 0.436 for the SimpleViT control; wrong-side attention share 0.331 against 0.824 and 0.254; the 2026-09-14 anchors reproduced to within 0.001). After the paper's two-stage fine-tuning with three seeds, **H1 passes**: the hallway C50 error of the oriented model (arm C) minus the SimpleViT control (arm A) is +0.002 dB with a two-way 95 % interval of [−0.057, +0.060] dB against the margin of +0.23 dB, where exp_02 had measured +0.92 dB for the plain cylindrical model. **H1b passes**: C minus the cylindrical model fine-tuned in the same heading frame without the channel (arm F) is −2.01 dB [−2.31, −1.72]. **H2** finds no detected difference between C and A in any of the eleven room × metric cells after the Bonferroni-11 adjustment; exp_02 had six cells lost by the cylindrical model and none won. **H3** (simulated unseen rooms, k = 0, five evaluation seeds) is *non-inferior on C50 only*: C versus the exp_01 cylindrical baseline (arm B) has ρ = +3.2 % on EDT with a one-sided upper bound of +4.4 %, outside the +3 % margin, and ρ = −0.4 % on C50 (upper +0.8 %); versus the SimpleViT control the oriented model is non-inferior on both (EDT −1.1 %, C50 −1.0 %, descriptive). ## Interpretation

The hallway deficit was a mirror ambiguity, and two constant orientation channels remove it. Zero-shot, the plain cylindrical model's C50 error on the far (+y) side of the corridor is 8.2 dB against 1.0 dB on the near side, because its azimuth-relative tokens cannot distinguish a microphone from its mirror image in a nearly 180°-symmetric panorama and the loudspeaker faces one end; the oriented model reads 3.0 dB against 1.2 dB, the SimpleViT 2.4 dB against 2.4 dB. After fine-tuning the oriented model sits at the control's level in every room and on every metric, while both arms that only rotate the scene into the heading frame get *worse* on the hallway (SimpleViT +0.63 dB, cylindrical +1.09 dB C50 against their room-frame selves): the frame is not the fix, the channel is, and the channel wins back more than the frame costs (C − D −0.63 dB). On the other rooms the frame effect is small and the oriented model neither helps nor harms.

The simulated result is the flip side. In exp_03 the cylindrical encoder's EDT advantage on simulated rooms came with tokens that ignore absolute azimuth; giving the pooled feature an absolute orientation reference returns EDT to within 1 % of the SimpleViT, and the pre-registered H3 rule marks the 3 % loss against the cylindrical baseline. C50 is unaffected in simulation. The trade is therefore explicit: the oriented pipeline matches the best real-room baseline everywhere and matches the SimpleViT in simulation, but it does not carry the cylindrical model's simulated EDT edge into the real rooms.
