# Analysis — yaw_rotation_degradation

## Outcome

**The claim that jointly rotating the panorama and the source–receiver coordinates in yaw substantially degrades xRIR's RIR synthesis is not supported, for the same-budget SimpleViT model and for the released checkpoint alike.** Under the pre-registered rule (Bonferroni-adjusted lower bound of the relative error increase above +10 % for EDT or C50 at any of nine non-zero angles, condition P), no cell passes. On this fixed split the adjusted query-level upper bounds for EDT and C50 stay below +10 % at every angle in all three models (largest +7.56 %, released C50 at −45°); at the room level (17 rooms resampled, same adjusted level) four C50 upper bounds exceed +10 % (control +22.5° +10.99 % and +45° +11.37 %, cylindrical +45° +10.25 %, released −45° +10.75 %), so a substantial effect over rooms in general is not ruled out, only not shown. The degradation that is measured is small and angle-shaped. C50: control +5.09 % at +45° [+2.65, +7.52] and +4.12 % at +22.5°, +4.55 % at −45°; cylindrical +5.34 % at −45° [+3.26, +7.37] and +5.19 % at +45°; released +4.70 % at −45° [+1.82, +7.56], while its +22.5° and +45° rows (+1.54 %, +2.43 %) have adjusted intervals crossing zero. EDT: largest +1.34 % (control, +45°, interval crossing zero), +2.36 % (released, −45°, crossing zero) and +3.07 % (cylindrical, −45° [+0.90, +5.44]). For the control and the cylindrical model EDT and C50 return to within ±1 % at ±5.6° and at 180°; the released model's EDT is −2.2 % at 180° and −2.9 % at 90°. T60 (descriptive) moves between −3.6 % (180°) and +1.0 % for the control and up to +5.2 % (−45°) for the released checkpoint. H2 (the cylindrical backbone degrades less) is not evaluable under its rule because H1 has no passing cell. Descriptively, D_k = r_cyl − r_control is positive at −45° for EDT with the adjusted query-level interval excluding zero (+2.23 % [+0.38, +4.17]); no adjusted query-level D_k for C50 excludes zero (the adjusted room-cluster intervals at −90° do, EDT [+0.23, +2.55] and C50 [+0.13, +1.72]); TOST equivalence of the cylindrical model's own r_k to zero within ±2 % is not established at any patch-aligned angle except 180° (C50 query-level; EDT room-level). Nothing here is a cross-model equivalence test.

## Reliability

- **Design.** All three models are evaluated on the same 6337 unseen queries with the same eight references per query, fixed by a hashed manifest; the 12-file source closure (the evaluator plus its 11 repo-local imports) is byte-identical to the reviewed commit `62c9107b4150e44c4ac410ff4cab359c1e71cc10` (source-closure binding in every run's provenance sidecar, verified post hoc: no later commits, every file last modified before the runs started); fp32 with TF32 off; every batch padded to the canonical size 16 (acoustic metrics exactly batch-shape independent under this protocol); per-query seeded Griffin-Lim phases. The rotation is exact: an integer column roll of the panorama plus the same rigid rotation of every coordinate, so the target is unchanged and every per-sample difference belongs to the model-plus-evaluation pipeline (subject to the unquantified CUDA run-to-run residual noted below). Statistics are paired per query with r_k computed inside each bootstrap resample; query-level and room-cluster intervals are both at the adjusted level; the second bootstrap seed moved no decision-driving bound by more than 3.8 % of its width. No acoustic metric became invalid at any angle (newly-invalid fraction 0.0000 everywhere), so there is no survivor selection.
- **Condition E versus condition P.** On the measured grid (four non-zero E angles × three acoustic metrics × three models) the end-to-end and pinned-alignment r_k agree to 2.0e-5 (computed from the canonical rows), and the mean loss agrees to 3e-6 at every one of the 18 angles. The delay-flip audit shows why: at most 4 of the 50 696 (query, reference) pairs change their integer direct-path delay at any angle. Under this protocol the measured contribution of the `shift_and_align` step is negligible on this grid; where the remaining change arises is not identified by this design (and an unquantified CUDA run-to-run residual remains). E was not evaluated acoustically at the other five angles.
- **k = 0 parity gate and its amendments.** The pre-registered band (2 × reference-draw spread measured on the control) failed twice: first on control EDT and cylindrical C50, then, after adding the measured unseeded-phase and TF32 spreads (both measured on the control and reused for the other models), on cylindrical C50 alone. Both amendments were pre-specified before the extra measurements and the failed decisions are kept in the record. The v3 band, band(model) = 2 × (S_ref(model) + S_phase + S_tf32 + S_shape(model)) + 1e-6, adds the cylindrical model's own reference-draw spread (manifest seeds 1–2) and its batch-1 versus batch-16 shape spread; S_shape for the control and the released model was not measured and enters as 0, and S_phase and S_tf32 remain control-only measurements. It passed 11/11; the largest measured discrepancy against exp_01 is 1.3 % (cylindrical C50), about 7.7 × below the 10 % threshold. The amendments widen the band on the basis of measured nuisance only and do not touch the yaw analysis, whose comparisons are within-run and within-manifest.
- **A finding from the gate.** The cylindrical model's k = 0 EDT and C50 move about 2.5 × more than the control's when the references are re-drawn (EDT 0.000311 s vs 0.000123 s, C50 0.0080 dB vs 0.0033 dB over manifest seeds 1–2; T60 and loss spreads are similar between the models), which is why the v1/v2 bands failed on its C50. The properly paired k = 0 comparison in this experiment (same references and phases for both models) gives cylindrical − control: EDT −4.4 % with both the query-level (−0.0025 to −0.0015 s) and the room-cluster (−0.0038 to −0.0001 s) intervals excluding zero, loss −1.5 % (both), log-STFT MSE −1.5 % (query only), T60 −1.3 % (query only), C50 no detected difference. These are nominal, unadjusted 95 % intervals on two single-seed checkpoints and are descriptive, not a pre-registered architecture result; the direction and size agree with exp_01 (EDT −3.7 %), and the room-level EDT interval clears zero only just.
- **Nuisance and residual risks (from the full code review, updated).** (i) CUDA deterministic algorithms are not pinned and the sweep was run once per model, so angle-specific run-to-run nondeterminism is an unquantified residual; the k = 0 gate bounds only the k = 0 discrepancy against exp_01. (ii) The environment (Python 3.8.20, torch 2.0.1+cu117, cuDNN 8500, driver 590.48.01, two RTX A6000) is recorded post hoc in the sidecars; the runs' own metadata records only the torch version. (iii) The decomposition covers the receiver-view tokens, the pooled feature and the query-source coordinate embedding on one batch of 16; the reference-coordinate features are not decomposed, and its four relative changes live in different, non-additive spaces.
- **Residual limitations (recorded, not fixable without re-running).** (i) Every provenance binding is post hoc: the source closure, the environment (Python/torch/CUDA/cuDNN/driver/GPUs), the content-hashed dataset inventory, the run-log digests and the chronology (start times read from log-file names and file modification times) were captured after the runs finished, so they prove that the retained bytes are consistent with each other and with the reviewed commit, not that exactly those bytes and that environment existed at execution time. (ii) Five gate sidecars (the launcher-v1 runs `gate_control`, `gate_cyl`, `gate_released`, `gate_noise_seed1`, `gate_noise_seed2`) carry a command template with a `<0|1>` GPU placeholder, and five (`gate_phase_seed1`, `gate_tf32`, `gate_noise_cyl_seed1`, `gate_noise_cyl_seed2`, `gate_shape_cyl_b1`) were written post hoc without a command; their commands in `_command.md` are reconstructions from the notebook and the logs. (iii) The retained launcher `launch_yaw_sweep_v3.sh` was never used for a real run (the gate and the sweep ran under v2); it has been exercised only by refusal self-tests and stub fault tests, and no isolated end-to-end gate → decision → binding → sweep run exists. (iv) CUDA run-to-run nondeterminism is unquantified (single pass per model). (v) The `delay_flips_max` denominator (6337 × 8) is not serialised because the evaluator's metadata carries no shot count. (vi) Binding chronology, from the retained binder logs: the three sweep sidecars were first bound by binder v2 at 20:20:18 (evaluator file only), all 13 sidecars by v3 at 21:06:20 (12-file closure), rebound by v4 at 21:45:06 (content-hashed data, logs, gate inputs) and touched by v5 at 22:47:24 (report migration only). Bindings became immutable with v6 (23:15:24), after those rebinds had overwritten the earlier `bound_at` values, so no sidecar carries a `binding_history` today (the list starts with the next migration, if any) and the report's `written` stamp is the v5 migration time; the earlier times survive only in the logs named here.
- **Scope.** One checkpoint per backbone (exp_01, epoch 12, one training seed each) and the released checkpoint; AcousticRooms unseen split only, with every training room axis-aligned; rotations exact at integer columns (0.70° per column); source and receiver both omnidirectional in the simulation, so the invariance being tested is a true symmetry of the target.

- **Open auxiliary tooling items (not part of the evidence path; recorded per the commit decision rule, Codex rounds 6–8).** (a) `launch_yaw_sweep_v3.sh`: the retained-copy steps after a gate decision are prose, not commands, and its decision command uses the working-tree producer while acceptance recomputes with the pinned commit; no clean end-to-end gate → decision → binding → sweep run has been executed with it (real evaluator-change, checkpoint-change and rename-failure guards unexercised). (b) The page and results.md generators refuse missing sections but still accept a single deleted row inside a section, an all-null TOST payload, and empty final `h1.bounds`/`h2.verdict` payloads; some Markdown column headings truncate the producer's metric names to fit the table (the full names follow in the same line). (c) The binder replaces files one by one after validating the whole transaction; the sequence is not crash-atomic across files, and a changed sidecar digest is accepted when the embedded old and new contents are identical. (d) No renderer-corruption or clean-launcher integration tests beyond those listed in the notebook. None of these can alter what this record shows: every canonical artefact is hash-bound, the acceptance checker recomputes the gate decision from the bound inputs, and the reviewer confirmed in round 8 that no malformed-input path could pass a different record as this one.

## Interpretation

The angular pattern is consistent with a diagonal-heading distribution-shift hypothesis rather than a general failure to handle rotation, but this design cannot separate the candidates: wall orientation relative to the training distribution, the periodicity of the positional encodings and of the learned position pool, and the absolute-coordinate embeddings all vary together with the angle. What the data show: every training room is axis-aligned in the receiver frame, so 90° and 180° yaws produce another axis-aligned scene while ±45° and ±135° produce wall azimuths never seen in training; for the control and the cylindrical model the spectral consistency (mean absolute change of the predicted log-spectrogram) peaks at ±45° / ±135° (control 0.127 / 0.135, cylindrical 0.114 / 0.119) and is roughly half as large at 90° and 180° (control 0.077 / 0.071, cylindrical 0.067 / 0.060); the loss and C50 increases peak at ±45° and vanish at 180°; the small-angle rows (±2.8° to ±11°) ramp smoothly. The released checkpoint does not follow the half-size pattern (0.161 / 0.170 at 90° / 180° versus 0.177–0.208 at diagonal headings) and is the least invariant of the three overall.

The cylindrical encoder's tokens are azimuth-equivariant to 2e-7 under a one-patch yaw, yet the pooled feature changes by 1.7 %, the sinusoidal embedding of the raw query-source coordinates by 73 % (it encodes absolute xyz, which the rotation moves), and the output log-spectrogram by 0.35 %; the full model's acoustic degradation is then equal to or slightly larger than the SimpleViT control's. The learned position pool (`lin_proj_0`) and the coordinate branches, which both backbones share, are therefore the candidate downstream sources of the residual; the one-batch decomposition cannot rank them, and a hold-one-branch-fixed or replacement ablation is needed to attribute the effect. This is consistent with the hypothesis exp_02 raised for the hallway result (pooling as the bottleneck) but does not test it. Because H2 was not evaluable, the confirmatory evidence says only that the cylindrical backbone was not shown to degrade less; the descriptive D_k rows say it degraded slightly more on EDT at −45°.

Practically, on this split a scene yaw costs at most about 5 % of C50 error at diagonal headings (largest point effect +5.34 %, largest adjusted query-level upper bound +7.56 %), roughly half the pre-registered "substantial" threshold; for scale, the k = 0 difference between the two backbones on the same metric is −0.8 % (C50) and on EDT −4.4 %. Whether that matters for deployment, and whether yaw augmentation would remove it, are hypotheses this experiment does not test.

## Recommended next steps

1. If heading robustness is a goal, test the augmentation hypothesis directly: fine-tune with random-yaw augmentation (exact roll plus coordinate rotation, `tools/yaw_rotation.py`) and re-run this evaluator; the ±45° C50 rows are the cells to watch.
2. Attribute the residual with hold-one-fixed ablations: (a) replace the learned position pool with an azimuth-invariant pool, (b) express the coordinate embeddings in the panorama's gauge, each separately, then re-run exp_03 (and exp_01 for the accuracy cost).
3. Repeat the k = 0 paired comparison with a second training seed per backbone, with a pre-registered rule, before quoting the EDT advantage at room level.
4. Quantify run-to-run nondeterminism by repeating one model's sweep at two angles with identical settings.
5. Housekeeping deferred from exp_02: seed the Griffin-Lim phase per query in `sim_to_real/eval_haa.py` and record `depth_variant` in its per-sample meta; add regression tests for `summarize_haa.py` and `summarize_epochs.py`.
