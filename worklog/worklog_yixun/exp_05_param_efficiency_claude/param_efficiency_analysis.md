## 1. What was tested

Six arms: SimpleViT and CylindricalViT encoders at three matched capacities — S (2 766 080 / 2 777 984 encoder parameters), M (19 703 296 / 19 750 912; the released configuration; the M pair is exp_01's control and cylindrical run, not the released weights) and L (43 709 184 / 43 780 608) — under one fixed recipe (12 epochs, effective batch 64, same optimizer and schedule). Outside the encoder the architecture is the baseline's; the only tier-dependent module besides the ViT is the source projection, whose input width follows the encoder dimension (non-encoder parameters 12 307 133 / 12 372 669 / 12 438 205 at S / M / L, exact integers in the parameter table). H1 asks whether the cylindrical curve dominates the SimpleViT curve at every tested tier (EDT and C50, K = 8); H2 asks whether a smaller cylindrical model reaches a larger SimpleViT's accuracy (Cyl-S vs Base-M, Cyl-M vs Base-L: TOST ±3 % or superiority), each cell scoped to its paired cohort (6 337 queries, 17 rooms in every cell).

## 2. Results

**H1 (K = 8): partial on EDT and partial on C50.** Relative change cylindrical − SimpleViT with adjusted two-sided intervals (family m = 6): EDT S −1.32 % [−2.97, +0.39], M −4.16 % [−5.39, −2.89], L −2.21 % [−3.55, −0.85]; C50 S +0.62 % [−1.04, +2.27], M −0.60 % [−1.91, +0.68], L −2.52 % [−3.76, −1.27]. The cylindrical encoder is superior on EDT at M and at L, not at S; superior on C50 only at L. The registered verdict per metric at the three tested tiers is therefore "partial" for both, never "dominance". Room-cluster intervals include zero except L C50. T60 (descriptive): cylindrical lower at M (9.44 vs 9.58 %) and L (9.37 vs 9.54 %), close at S (9.64 vs 9.63 %).

**H1 (K = 1): not supported on EDT and not supported on C50.** With a single reference every point estimate favours the SimpleViT: EDT S +4.50 % [+3.60, +5.41], M +2.18 % [+1.58, +2.77], L +0.58 % [−0.03, +1.20] (the L interval includes zero); C50 S +1.18 % [+0.69, +1.68], M +2.76 % [+2.40, +3.12], L +0.81 % [+0.42, +1.19]. This is the single-reference reversal exp_03 found at M, seen here at all three tiers; the EDT reversal is largest at S, the C50 reversal largest at M.

**H2 (K = 8), one four-cell family, each cell on its paired cohort of 6 337 queries / 17 rooms.** Cyl-M vs Base-L (encoder ratio 0.452, 2.21× fewer encoder parameters): superior on EDT (−4.38 % [−5.68, −3.10]) and on C50 (−3.46 % [−4.74, −2.13]); both cells reach the target, so the 2.21× ratio is published. Cyl-S vs Base-M (ratio 0.141, 7.09×): reaches the target on EDT by equivalence (−1.35 % [−2.72, +0.04], TOST interval inside ±3 %) and on C50 by equivalence with a worse point estimate (+1.74 % [+0.41, +3.07], TOST inside ±3 %); both cells reach, so the 7.09× ratio is published. **H2 (K = 1):** Cyl-S vs Base-M reaches neither cell; Cyl-M vs Base-L reaches C50 only, by equivalence (+2.05 %), not EDT (+3.02 %) — no K = 1 parameter ratio is published.

**Observed curve shape (three points per backbone, one training seed each, fixed budget).** The SimpleViT points are 47.00 / 47.02 / 47.13 ms EDT and 1.252 / 1.238 / 1.275 dB C50 at S / M / L, i.e. no improvement with encoder size at this budget; the cylindrical points are 46.38 / 45.06 / 46.08 ms, with the largest paired margin at M. Whether the SimpleViT would improve with a longer budget or at other capacities is not tested; the model's output is a weighted combination of the reference spectrograms, which is one hypothesis for why the encoder size matters little here.

**Yaw robustness (descriptive, seed 42, K = 8, headings k ∈ {32, 64, 448, 480} of 512 versus k = 0).** Across the six arms and four rotations the change ranges from −0.27 % to +3.24 % on EDT and from +2.32 % to +5.77 % on C50; the largest costs are at the diagonal headings for both backbones. No consistent advantage for the cylindrical encoder was observed at any tier in this single-seed descriptive sweep (no robustness claim in either direction is established by it).

## 3. Reading for the paper

At the released capacity (M) the cylindrical encoder improves EDT by 4.2 % at matched parameter count, and Cyl-M is superior to a SimpleViT with 2.2× more encoder parameters on both EDT and C50; Cyl-S is comparable (±3 %) to the SimpleViT at the released capacity with 7.1× fewer encoder parameters. These statements hold on the unseen split at K = 8 for the tested tiers and one training seed per arm; they do not establish capacity saturation, a causal attribution to the positional code, generalisation across training seeds or to untested capacities, yaw equivalence, any K = 1 efficiency ratio, or full-system or runtime speed-ups (the ratios are encoder parameter ratios only). At K = 1 the SimpleViT is ahead at every tier.

## 4. Limitations and disclosures

None of these affects the objective, only wall time. The L tier fit at micro-batch 32 × 2 with ≈ 44.4 GiB peak reserved.
